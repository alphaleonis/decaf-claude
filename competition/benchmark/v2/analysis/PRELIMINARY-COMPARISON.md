# Preliminary tool comparison — v2 pilot data (nib `dcc-vkeh`)

**Preliminary. Two subjects, both `library`.** This is not the cross-subject comparison the corpus was
designed for — that needs the full run across size × application type. What follows is what the
pilot's 35 cells legitimately support, with every rule from
[`PILOT-RESULTS.md`](PILOT-RESULTS.md) applied.

Generated by `scoring/aggregate_pilot.py`, which refuses rather than emits when a rule fires. No
figure here was hand-computed.

## What the data actually supports

### 1. No tool wins on every axis, and the axes disagree

`comprehensive-review` has the best precision. `ours-audit` finds the most real defects (37) and the
most nobody else found (6), at the worst precision and the highest cost. `superpowers` is within
striking distance of both on substance while costing **a seventh of `ours-audit`** — $17.16 against
$117.60 for 31 real findings against 37.

A single "quality" score would rank these in an order none of the underlying evidence supports. That
is why the axes are reported separately and why `METHODOLOGY-v2` forbids merging them.

### 2. The low-volume tools are not measurable on precision, but they are informative

`anthropic-code-review` reported **11 clusters across both subjects and all 11 were real** — a perfect
record with no ratio attached, because n=11 split across two subjects is 7 and 4. `ours-bugs`
reported 7 and 5 were real. Both sit at the bottom of thread recall (0.15, 0.05).

What can be said: these two say very little, almost all of it is right, and they miss most of what
expert human reviewers raised. What cannot be said: a precision figure.

### 3. False positives do not differentiate the field

**One false positive across all 28 pooled cells** (`ours-audit`), and three across all 793 findings in
the entire pilot including the null arm. These tools do not invent defects. The differentiator is
immateriality — trivia and valid-minor findings — not error.

### 4. Cost per real finding spans 12×

$0.55 (`superpowers`) to $6.92 (`ours-bugs`). The three decaf presets occupy $3.18–$6.92; the two
cheapest tools in the roster are both external. Cost is the axis with the widest and most stable
separation in the pilot.

### 5. The null arm reframes what precision means

On a change selected as having no known defect, precision ranged 0.38–0.83 — overlapping heavily with
the 0.46–1.00 seen on subjects with real defects. Precision measures whether a claim is *defensible*,
not whether the bug was *found*. Only the thread axis answers the second question, and on that axis
the spread is 0.05 to 0.60.

## What this comparison may not be used for

- **Any claim about application types other than `library`.** Both pooled subjects are libraries.
  The corpus's whole design is size × application type; this covers one cell of that grid at two
  sizes.
- **A ranking presented as settled.** Two subjects, and the precision ranges overlap for four of the
  five measurable tools.
- **A precision figure for `anthropic-code-review` or `ours-bugs`.** Withheld deliberately.
- **Naming which threads the field missed.** The count reproduces across grading passes; the identity
  does not.
- **Pooling with the null arm or with any in-window subject.** The aggregator refuses.

## Method

```
python3 competition/benchmark/v2/scoring/aggregate_pilot.py \
  competition/benchmark/v2/pooled/prometheus-prometheus-18081 \
  competition/benchmark/v2/pooled/dotnet-efcore-34127 \
  competition/benchmark/v2/null/grafana-122269 \
  -o pilot-data.json
```

Enforced by that script, exiting 3 rather than emitting a number: the n ≥ 10 ratio floor, medians
across grading passes, `vintage.check_pooling` against mixing in-window and out-of-window subjects,
and separation of the null arm. The single-application-type limitation is emitted into the data as
`scope.single_app_type` so a downstream consumer cannot infer breadth from the subject count.
