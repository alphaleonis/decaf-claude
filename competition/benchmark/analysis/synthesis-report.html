Controlled benchmark · findings-quality analysis

Finding the bug is table stakes. The gap is everything else you have to read.

Five code-review tools, nine merged-then-reverted pull requests, two repeats each — every finding clustered and graded blind against a frozen answer key. On six of nine subjects almost every tool found the escaped bug, so recall barely separates the field. What separates it is the ratio of substantive findings to noise, and the price paid per substantive finding — which spans 5× across the field.

Read this column-wise, not row-wise. On subjects 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 and 10 the escaped bug was found by 9 or 10 of the 10 cells — those six subjects rank nobody. The entire recall spread comes from three columns: subject 4, where only anthropic caught it (its git-history agent pulled the revert commit; the defect was a breaking change to a public API's token stream, invisible in the diff and absent from the project's own test suite), subject 9, and subject 5.

This is a null result, and worth stating plainly. No tool hallucinates defects at a rate that would disqualify it, and the ordering here is nearly the inverse of the quality ordering above — superpowers has the highest FP share precisely because its denominator is small, while its absolute rate (0.28/run) ties for the lowest.

Share and rate disagree, so read both. The tools that fire most often in absolute terms — ours at 0.78 and tag1 at 0.89 false positives per run — are the two with the largest rosters. Several of those were traps the answer keys planted deliberately: diff patterns that look like defects but are documented intent.

Severity calibration is P(judged substantive | the tool called it critical or high). At 0.92, nine of ten things anthropic escalates are real — you can read its top of list and stop. At 0.47–0.55 a reader has to check everything, which erases much of the value of having a severity field at all.

One caveat that cuts against the leader: anthropic does not emit severities — it emits 0–100 confidence scores and hard-filters below 80. Its calibration is therefore computed over a smaller, self-selected set than the tools that rank a full ladder, and is not weight-comparable. The gap is real; its exact size is not.

Ours costs 2.8× anthropic and 8.7× superpowers per run, and produces the lower-quality mix of the three. It also takes 31.7 minutes of wall clock against anthropic's 15.0 — for a reviewer waiting on feedback, that is the difference between a coffee and a context switch.

Sub-agent count does not predict quality either: anthropic runs 11.8 agents to ours' 14.5 and beats it on every quality axis, while superpowers reaches 39% substantive with one agent. What the extra agents reliably buy is output volume, which is the thing being paid for.

The medium row is the weakest, and it is two subjects — one of which is a deliberate trap where the obvious-looking defect had already been fixed before merge. Do not over-read it.

Conclusions

Five tools, five distinct roles — and one of them is not worth its price.

Best overall

anthropic /code-review

Leads on every quality axis and is cheaper than three of the four others. The only tool that caught every escaped bug, including subject 4's public-API break that required reasoning about consumers outside the diff. Its confidence filter is what produces the clean output — though on one subject that same filter discarded a true primary-bug facet its own agents had found.

Method & caveats

What this measures, and what it deliberately does not conclude.

  • Subjects are merged-then-reverted PRs from dotnet, microsoft, prometheus, kubernetes and BurntSushi. Each has a frozen answer key naming the escaped defect, any human-review issues, and known-safe traps — diff patterns that look like defects but are documented intent. Keys were written before any tool output was read.
  • Grading is blind and tool-blind. Findings are pooled into clusters, shuffled, and stripped of tool identity before the judge sees them. Every false-positive and valid-other verdict must be justified against the diff.
  • No language axis. There is one subject per language × size cell, so a single odd PR would masquerade as a language effect. Size pools 3–4 subjects and is reported; language is not, and should not be inferred from the per-subject pages.
  • Recall is saturated and the ranking is thin. Six of nine subjects were solved by almost every cell. The bug-catch column is decided by two or three hard PRs — read it as suggestive, not settled.
  • Calibration is not weight-comparable. Anthropic emits confidence scores rather than severities and hard-filters below 80, so its 0.92 rests on a smaller self-selected set than tools ranking a full severity ladder.
  • Two repeats per cell. Enough to expose stochasticity, not enough for a confidence interval. A one-of-two result means exactly that.
  • Judge and grading rubric are in-house. Blind and tool-blind, but the definition of "substantive" may still favour a particular reporting style. Verdicts are committed per subject for audit.
  • Three subjects remain unrun (go/medium, rust/medium, rust/large), and per-tool figures may shift when they land.
