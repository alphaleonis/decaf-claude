## Specialist Notes

### Considered But Not Flagged (all agents)

**Tiered down by the evidence/confidence gate (recorded, not counted):**

- `extensions/copilot/src/platform/networking/common/fetcherService.ts:346` — **Low, telemetry attribution** (adversarial-reviewer, confidence 50, single finder). The watchdog calls `stream.destroy()` → `reader.cancel()` with **no reason**. Downstream, the `Response` transform stream's `cancel(undefined)` records the teardown as `outcome:'cancel'`, making an idle-timeout teardown indistinguishable from a genuine user cancellation — so reliability telemetry can undercount hung endpoints as cancellations. Suggested fix: thread a distinguishing reason (e.g. `stream.destroy(new StreamIdleTimeoutError(...))` → `reader.cancel(reason)`) so idle timeouts surface as `outcome:'error'`. Tiered down because a single-finder Low at anchor 50 does not clear the `bugs`/`evidence=strong` bar (≥80, or ≥60 with 2+ finders) and falls under the confidence gate (below anchor 75); it is an observability improvement, not a correctness defect in the changed lines. Worth a follow-up but not a merge blocker.

**Hypotheses examined and refuted (adversarial + broad + typescript reviewers agree):**

- **Cancel rejection masking the thrown timeout error** — refuted. Per WHATWG Streams, `reader.cancel()` fulfills the pending `read()` with `{done:true}` rather than rejecting; the loop breaks normally, reaches the post-`finally` `throw`, and `StreamIdleTimeoutError` propagates. Confirmed by the passing "first chunk never arrives" spec.
- **Post-loop `throw` sitting outside the `try/finally`** — correct by design. On the timeout path the loop exits via a natural `done:true` break and falls through to the `throw`; on a consumer `break`/`return`/`maybeCancel` the generator is driven via `.return()`, which runs `finally` and completes without reaching the throw (and `timedOut` is false there anyway). This is intended: an explicit consumer stop is not a timeout.
- **Wrong `timeoutMs`/message after N chunks** — refuted. On a first-chunk timeout the `isFirstChunk = false` line is never reached (the break precedes it), so the reported timeout/message always matches the timer that actually fired. Both the "first chunk" and "inactivity" spec assertions confirm this.
- **Unhandled rejection at the `AsyncIterableObject` call sites** (messagesApi/responsesApi) — refuted. The executor wrapper (`util/vs/base/common/async.ts` ~2031-2036) catches the throw and routes it to `reject()`, so the consumer's `next()` rethrows it cleanly.
- **Double `destroy()` (watchdog teardown + `_onReturn`) crashing** — refuted. `_onReturn`'s `destroy()` is not called on the error/throw path; a second `destroy()` after the reader is released falls through to a no-op `stream.cancel()`.
- **`releaseLock()` throwing on a pending read during `iterator.return()`** — refuted for this environment: `iterator.return()` runs only after a value was yielded (no pending read), and modern Node permits `releaseLock()` with pending reads regardless.
- **Boundary race — chunk arrives at the deadline, is yielded, then a spurious timer tears the stream down** — refuted. The `read()` resolution is a microtask draining before the `setTimeout` macrotask, so `clearTimer()` cancels the pending timer first.
- **`void stream.destroy().catch(() => {})` as a floating promise** — not a defect. The `.catch()` observes the rejection; fire-and-forget cleanup is intentional and subordinate to the thrown timeout error.

**Out of reach (narrow):**

- Whether downstream retry logic treats `StreamIdleTimeoutError` as retryable (and could re-arm the 2-minute first-chunk wait) — a plausible cascade the adversarial reviewer could not make concrete from the files in reach; not reported.
